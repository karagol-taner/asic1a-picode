CC   -!- FUNCTION: Forms voltage-independent, pH-gated trimeric sodium channels
CC       that act as postsynaptic excitatory receptors in the nervous system,
CC       playing a crucial role in regulating synaptic plasticity, learning, and
CC       memory (PubMed:21036899, PubMed:32915133, PubMed:34319232). Upon
CC       extracellular pH drop this channel elicits transient, fast activating,
CC       and completely desensitizing inward currents (PubMed:21036899).
CC       Displays high selectivity for sodium ions but can also permit the
CC       permeation of other cations (PubMed:21036899). Regulates more or less
CC       directly intracellular calcium concentration and CaMKII
CC       phosphorylation, and thereby the density of dendritic spines. Modulates
CC       neuronal activity in the circuits underlying innate fear (By
CC       similarity). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q6NXK8,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:21036899, ECO:0000269|PubMed:32915133,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:34319232}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Isoform Asic1a]: Has high selectivity for sodium ions, but
CC       can also be permeable to other cations including calcium, lithium and
CC       potassium. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:21036899}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Isoform Asic1b]: Produces acid activated currents with a
CC       reduced amplitude and inactivates faster (PubMed:21036899). Has high
CC       selectivity for sodium ions but also supports a calcium-mediated
CC       current which is sustained and maintained as long as acidic conditions
CC       are present (PubMed:21036899). Also potentially permeable to lithium
CC       and potassium (PubMed:21036899). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:21036899}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [Isoform 1]: Has no measurable proton-gated sodium channel
CC       activity in vitro. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:21036899}.
